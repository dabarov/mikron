How it works

  1. 1

    Hold Shift

    Move the mouse a little. The cursor turns into a crosshair and a hint follows it. You can pick Alt, Ctrl or Cmd instead.

  2. 2

    Drag over anything

    Padding and gaps become hatched bands with their values. Boxes get a width and height. Rounded corners get a radius.

  3. 3

    Release, then Copy

    Let go to pin the selection. Copy takes the numbers with you. Esc, or a click elsewhere, dismisses it.

Copy the numbers, not a screenshot

Copy gives you everything inside the selection as text: one line per element, with its size, padding, gap, radius, background and font. Paste it into a ticket, a pull request or an AI assistant.

The default. Identical siblings collapse into one line with a count.

How to read the spec:
- One line per element. Indentation is DOM nesting. The name is the tag with its #id and .classes, then the visible text in quotes.
- "W × H" is the border-box size of the whole element.
- padding and radius are in CSS shorthand order (top right bottom left; radius starts at the top-left corner).
- gap is the space between the element's children ("x / y" when the two axes differ).
- font is size/line-height, weight and family. bg and color are computed values.
- "×N" after a name means N identical siblings in a row. "(partial)" means the selection cut through the element.

It only acts on Shift + drag

  • Typing is safe. Shift with a letter, Shift+Tab and shortcuts never start it.
  • Text fields, sliders, maps, code editors, canvas and video are left alone.
  • If a page claims the mouse press for itself, Mikron backs off.
  • Switch it off for one site, or everywhere with Alt+Shift+M.

Nothing leaves your browser

  • Mikron reads layout only while you draw a selection, on your device.
  • While installed it makes no network requests. No analytics, no remote code.
  • Every line is public under the Apache 2.0 licence, with a test suite.
  • The privacy policy is short and says what each permission is for.

What it does not do

Mikron measures the top frame of a page: the inside of an iframe is drawn as one box, and so are canvas and video. It needs a mouse or trackpad, so there is no touch gesture. Browser pages such as chrome:// and the Chrome Web Store do not allow extensions at all.
